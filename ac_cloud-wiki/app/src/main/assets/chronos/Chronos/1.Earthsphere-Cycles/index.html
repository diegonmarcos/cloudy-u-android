Earthsphere - Cycles

Day/night terminator, seasons, Moon and tides, and the Milankovitch orbital cycles. Self-contained, no external libraries.

Day / night terminator

cos z = sinφ sinδ + cosφ cosδ cos H   |   sinδ = sinε sinλ   |   H = local hour angle (UTC + longitude + equation of time). Dark = Sun below horizon; amber = subsolar point, grey = sub-lunar point.

Seasons: orbit and axial tilt

Orbit drawn with exaggerated eccentricity. r = a(1-e²)/(1+e cosν), ν = λ - ϖ. Tilt ε from the epoch slider.

Insolation by latitude (daily mean, top of atmosphere)

Q = (S0/π)(1+e cosν)²/(1-e²)² [h0 sinφ sinδ + cosφ cosδ sin h0], cos h0 = -tanφ tanδ, S0 = 1361 W/m²

Insolation through the year at chosen latitude

Same formula, evaluated for every day of the year; solar declination shown dashed (scaled). Vertical line = selected date.

Moon phase and tides

Synodic month 29.530589 d, reference new Moon 2000-01-06 18:14 UTC. Illuminated fraction = (1 - cos α)/2. Tide range ∝ √(1 + k² + 2k cos 2α), k = 0.46 (solar/lunar tidal force).

Milankovitch cycles

Sum-of-sinusoid approximation of the Berger (1978) solutions, for illustration: e = 0.0275 + 0.0095 cos(2πt/100 kyr + p1) + 0.0075 cos(2πt/413 kyr + p2) (eccentricity, ~100 and ~413 kyr); ε = 23.4° + 0.9° cos(2π(t+10.7)/41 kyr) (obliquity ~41 kyr); climatic precession e sinϖ, ϖ advancing 360° per ~21 kyr (axial precession alone 25.77 kyr). The last curve is the 21 June daily insolation at 65°N, computed with the insolation formula above. t = kyr from present (negative = past). Marker follows the epoch slider.
