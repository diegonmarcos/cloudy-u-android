2.0 FJK (Forger-Jewett-Kronauer) Model

Systemic Pacemaker

Simulates the core SCN circadian pacemaker amplitude alongside the endogenous melatonin curve (DLMO marker) acting as the systemic time-keeper.

Fixed Model Assumptions & Constants

Intrinsic Period (τ): Fixed at ~24.2h (human average). Requires light pulses for daily 24h entrainment.

PRC Limits: Type 1 resetting bounds maximum phase shift amplitude per photic event.

Phase Angles: DLMO is biologically locked to precede CBTmin by exactly ~7.0 - 7.5 hours.

2.1 Borbély Two-Process Model

Arousal/Sleep Dynamics

Modeling homeostatic sleep pressure (Process S) accumulation during wakefulness and decay during sleep, juxtaposed with SCN-driven arousal (Process C).

Fixed Model Assumptions & Constants

Accumulation (τrise): Process S exponential rise time constant fixed at 18.2h during wakefulness.

Decay (τdecay): Process S exponential decay time constant fixed at 4.2h during slow-wave sleep.

Process C Alignment: Assumes a perfect sinusoidal arousal curve phase-locked 12 hours away from CBTmin.

2.2 Leloup-Goldbeter Model

Cellular TTFL

Approximate concentrations of activator complexes (CLOCK:BMAL1) versus inhibitory repressors (PER:CRY) within SCN neurons (the molecular feedback loop).

Fixed Model Assumptions & Constants

Enzyme Kinetics: Fixed Vmax and Km constants for PER/CRY degradation (via Casein Kinase 1).

Translational Delays: Fixed temporal delay between mRNA transcription and cytoplasmic protein accumulation.

Peripheral Uncoupling: Assumes liver/gut TTFL machinery is identical to SCN but can be phase-shifted autonomously by delayed nutrient timing.

2.3 Physio-Hybrid Model

Wearable ML Metrics

Translates circadian and stress cues into observable peripheral biometrics: Heart Rate (spiking during allostatic stress) and peripheral Skin Temperature.

Fixed Model Assumptions & Constants

Autonomic Shift: Sleep onset instantly triggers parasympathetic dominance (bradycardia, vasodilation).

Allostatic Decay: Acute sympathetic stressors decay at a fixed exponential rate over time.

Dietary Thermogenesis: Fixed HR/Temp bump multipliers based on primary macronutrient digestion (e.g., higher thermal cost for carbs/proteins vs. fats).

2.4 Endocrine & Hormonal Rhythm

Circadian System

Displays the multi-hormonal cascade orchestrated by the SCN and peripheral oscillators over the diurnal cycle.

Endocrine Triggers

Cortisol: Exhibits the CAR (Cortisol Awakening Response) spike right after wakefulness, governed by HPA axis and blood baseline.

Growth Hormone (GH): Pulses strongly 1-2 hours into nocturnal sleep (Slow-Wave N3 Phase).

Insulin & Leptin: Insulin spikes coordinate with theoretical meal times based on frequency; Leptin rises inversely to daylight hours to suppress nocturnal hunger.
